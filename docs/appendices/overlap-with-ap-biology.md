# Overlap with AP Biology

This appendix identifies concepts in this textbook that also appear in the
College Board's AP Biology Curriculum and Exam Description (CED). Students
using this textbook to prepare for the AP Biology exam should pay particular
attention to these topics.

AP Biology organizes its evolution content primarily under **Big Idea 1:
Processes of Evolution Drive the Diversity and Unity of Life**, with
additional overlap in genetics, ecology, and molecular biology units.

## Partial Overlap — Other AP Biology Units

These concepts appear prominently in this textbook but are distributed across
multiple AP Biology units beyond Big Idea 1.

### Genetics and Molecular Biology (Units 5–6)

| Concept | Relevance to Evolution |
|---|---|
| Mutations (point, frameshift, chromosomal) | Source of heritable variation |
| Crossing over and recombination | Increases genetic diversity |
| Gene regulation | Enables developmental plasticity and adaptation |
| Horizontal gene transfer | Major force in prokaryotic evolution |
| Transposable elements | Source of genomic variation |

### Ecology (Unit 8)

| Concept | Relevance to Evolution |
|---|---|
| Coevolution (predator–prey, host–parasite, mutualism) | Reciprocal natural selection |
| Niche and competitive exclusion | Drives character displacement and speciation |
| Island biogeography | Illustrates speciation and extinction dynamics |
| Keystone species and trophic cascades | Evolutionary context for community structure |

### Cell Biology and Biochemistry (Units 1–4)

| Concept | Relevance to Evolution |
|---|---|
| Endosymbiotic theory (origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts) | Major evolutionary transition |
| Protein structure shaped by natural selection | Molecular evolution |
| Enzyme function and active site conservation | Evidence for common ancestry |

## AP Biology Exam Tips

- **Free-response questions** frequently ask students to interpret phylogenetic
  trees, apply Hardy-Weinberg to population data, or design experiments
  testing natural selection. All three are covered in depth in this textbook.
- **Multiple-choice questions** on evolution often feature novel scenarios
  (e.g., antibiotic resistance, Galápagos finch beak data). This textbook's
  case studies are designed to build that flexible reasoning.
- Evolution is the **unifying theme** of AP Biology — even units on cell
  biology and genetics include an evolutionary lens. Understanding the
  material in this textbook will strengthen performance across all AP Biology
  units, not just Unit 7.

## AP Biology Units Not Covered Here

The following AP Biology units have minimal or no overlap with this textbook:

- Unit 1: Chemistry of Life
- Unit 2: Cell Structure and Function
- Unit 3: Cellular Energetics (photosynthesis, cellular respiration)
- Unit 4: Cell Communication and Cell Cycle
- Unit 5: Heredity (Mendelian genetics, non-Mendelian inheritance)
- Unit 6: Gene Expression and Regulation (beyond evolutionary context)

Students preparing for the full AP Biology exam should use this textbook
alongside a comprehensive AP Biology resource for those units.
